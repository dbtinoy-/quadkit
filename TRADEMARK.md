# Quadkit Trademark Policy

The Quadkit source code is licensed under the [Apache License, Version
2.0](./LICENSE). **The Quadkit name and logo are not.**

This distinction is deliberate and is common practice in open source
(Rust, Python, Kubernetes, Grafana, and many others do the same). The code
is free for anyone to use, modify, and redistribute — including
commercially. The *name* is what lets users tell the official project apart
from everything else, so it is held back.

## What the marks are

- The word mark **"Quadkit"**, alone or as part of a longer name.
- The Quadkit logo and other project visual identity.
- Confusingly similar variants (for example "Quadkits", "Quadkit.js",
  "QuadKit", "Quad-Kit").

## What you may do without asking

You do **not** need permission to:

- **Use the software** for any purpose, commercial or otherwise. That is
  the Apache-2.0 grant and this policy does not narrow it.
- **Say your product uses Quadkit** — descriptive, truthful references
  such as "built with Quadkit", "powered by Quadkit", or "compatible
  with Quadkit" are fine, provided they do not imply that the project
  publishes, sponsors, or endorses your product.
- **Write about Quadkit** — books, blog posts, talks, tutorials, courses,
  and training material may use the name to refer to the project.
- **Distribute unmodified copies** of the software under its own name.
- **Name a plugin or integration** using the form
  "<Your Name> for Quadkit" or "<Your Name> Quadkit integration". Do not
  use the form "Quadkit <Something>", which reads as an official project.

## What requires permission

Please ask first if you want to:

- Use "Quadkit" in the name of your **product, company, service, or
  domain** (for example "Quadkit Cloud", "Quadkit Hosting",
  "quadkit-hosting.com").
- Use the name or logo in a way that suggests **official status,
  affiliation, partnership, or endorsement**.
- Use the logo as your own application, company, or service icon.
- Sell merchandise bearing the marks.

## Modified versions

If you distribute a **modified** version of Quadkit, you must not present
it as the official project. Specifically:

- Do not use "Quadkit" as the primary name of your fork. Pick your own
  name.
- You may state, factually, that your work is "a fork of Quadkit" or
  "derived from Quadkit".
- Do not use the Quadkit logo for the fork.
- Remove or clearly distinguish project branding so users are not misled
  about the origin of the software and know where to direct support
  requests.

This is the one restriction that matters most to us. Apache-2.0 gives you
every right to fork the code; this policy only prevents a fork from being
*mistaken* for the original.

## Package namespaces

The `quadkit` and `quadkit-*` names on PyPI, and the `quadkit`
organization on GitHub, are reserved for official project releases. Please
do not publish packages under names that imply they are official. Prefer a
suffix form such as `<yourname>-quadkit-<feature>`.

## Attribution

Apache-2.0 already requires that you retain the `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`
files when redistributing. Please keep the `NOTICE` file intact; you may
append your own notices to it, but do not remove existing ones.

## Reserved rights

The project reserves all rights in the marks not expressly granted here.
This policy may be updated; the version in the repository at the time you
use the marks is the one that applies.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether your use is permitted, open an issue and ask. We
would much rather answer a question early than send a complaint later. In
practice, honest descriptive use is almost always fine.
